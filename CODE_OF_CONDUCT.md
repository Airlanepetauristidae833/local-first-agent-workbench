# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want participation in Local-First Agent Workbench to be safe, constructive, and
welcoming. Contributors, maintainers, and users are expected to treat one another with
respect regardless of experience, identity, background, or viewpoint.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and code on their merits.
- Give specific, actionable feedback and receive it in good faith.
- Make room for questions and different levels of experience.
- Respect privacy, consent, attribution, and project security boundaries.
- Correct mistakes openly and help keep discussions focused on the project.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, personal attacks, or sexualized attention.
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Deliberately exposing credentials, private data, or security-sensitive reproduction
  details.
- Sustained disruption, intimidation, impersonation, or bad-faith manipulation.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, release
coordination, and other spaces where a person is representing this project.

## Reporting

Do not report conduct concerns in a public issue when doing so could expose a target,
witness, private conversation, or sensitive evidence. Send a private report through the
repository's [GitHub Security Advisory form](https://github.com/Joviei/local-first-agent-workbench/security/advisories/new)
and begin the title with `Code of Conduct`. Include only the information needed to assess
the report.

Maintainers will limit disclosure to people needed to review and resolve the matter. No
fixed response time is promised, but reports will be handled as promptly and impartially
as the maintainers' availability allows. A maintainer who is directly involved in a report
must not be its sole reviewer.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, request a change in behavior, issue a warning,
temporarily restrict participation, or permanently remove a participant from project
spaces. The response will be proportionate to the behavior, its impact, prior incidents,
and the need to protect the community.

Good-faith disagreement with a technical decision is not a conduct violation. Knowingly
false reports, retaliation, or disclosure intended to harm another person may themselves
result in enforcement.
